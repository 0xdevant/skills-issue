# 語氣分寸 — the register dial

Register is **[PREFERENCE]**, not correctness. There is no globally right level; there is only
the right level for this voice, audience and medium. What this file gives you is a shared
vocabulary so "make it less crude" means something specific.

**Say it in Cantonese: 斯文程度.** The dial is how 斯文 a voice is, framed positively. 搶口 is the
word for one line pitched too strong for its audience, so it names a symptom rather than the scale.
Either way, do not calque English "spicy" or "hot" into something like 「辣度」, which reads
translated.

**This is a tune, not a fact.** There is no correct level, only the level that fits the persona. A
dictionary cannot settle where you sit on this dial, and looking one up will not help: 搶口 names
the concept, it does not adjudicate it. The only real default is that you normally do not want to
sound rude. Everything past that is the voice's call, and the writer's own rules outrank this file.

## The five levels

| | Level | Where it lives | Licenses | Never |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **L1** | 正式 formal | Government, corporate comms, legal, hard news | 書面語 constructions where genuinely needed; full forms | Slang, 語氣詞 clusters, sarcasm |
| **L2** | 專業口語 professional spoken | Brand accounts, explainers, industry commentary | Full spoken Cantonese, dry wit, sharp words aimed at bad actors | 粗口, insults at products or readers, 硬膠-tier slang |
| **L3** | 輕鬆 casual | Vlogs, community posts, newsletters with a person behind them | Mild slang (黐線, 癲), self-deprecation, 語氣詞 freely | 粗口, punching down at named people |
| **L4** | 玩嘢 comedy / edgy | Comedy channels, personality-driven content where the persona owns it | 廢柴, 硬膠, on9, 仆街 as interjection — *if* the persona has earned it | 粗口 五大, targeting private individuals |
| **L5** | 粗口 | Personal speech, fiction dialogue | 屌/撚/柒/𨳍/閪 | Any branded or client-facing content |

Most brand and explainer content sits at **L2**. Most personality content sits at **L3**.
L4 is a deliberate choice a persona makes and sustains, not a place you drift into for one line.

### The table above is not the whole level

Both columns list sharp words, because that is where the risk of getting it wrong lives. Read only
those and you would think a higher level just means more ways to insult people. It does not. Each
level has an ordinary, neutral register that carries most of the writing:

| | Neutral markers at this level |
|---|---|
| **L1** | 敬請、並非、因此 |
| **L2** | 值得留意、不過、認真 |
| **L3** | 搞掂、咁啱、正斗 |
| **L4** | 神級、笑死、頂唔順 |
| **L5** | 粗口 as an intensifier, including on praise |

Pick the level from how the neutral writing should sound. The sharp-word columns then tell you what
that level does and does not license when you reach for something stronger.

## The two tests

A word being "allowed at this level" is necessary, not sufficient. Both tests must pass.

### 1. Target

**Sharp words describe a bad actor's behaviour. Never a product, a neutral party, or the reader.**

This matters more than the word's raw intensity. 陰濕 is a genuinely harsh word, and it works fine
aimed at a scam technique:

```
✓ 最陰濕係，成件事唔使偷密碼。          ← aimed at the attacker's method
✗ 最陰濕係佢鑲埋落 Docs 同 Slides。      ← aimed at a feature the writer admires
```

The second is broken not because 陰濕 is too strong, but because the target is wrong. The sentence
says "this is sneaky and contemptible" about something the writer is recommending.

### 2. Reshare

**If a reader forwarded this line to their own feed or a work group, would it embarrass them?**

This catches what the target test misses: lines that are correctly aimed but still make the reader
look bad for having passed them on. Especially useful for L3→L4 drift.

## The 「最X係」 pivot

A workhorse Cantonese move: it marks the single most striking fact in a piece. **[PREFERENCE]**,
but a very productive one — it forces you to decide what the point actually is.

```
最癲係 · 最荒謬係 · 最陰濕係 · 最實際係 · 最值得望係 · 最精彩嘅位 · 最狠嘅一招係
```

The filler you choose sets the register more than almost any other word in the sentence. 最癲係
is L2-safe; 最on9係 is L4. If you can't find a 「最X係」 for a piece, you may not have found the
story yet.

## Adjusting register without rewriting

The cheapest levers, roughly in order:

1. **語氣詞 density.** 啦/㗎/囉/喎/添 push casual. Stripping them pushes formal — too far, and it
   reads like a news bulletin.
2. **Verb choice.** 熄 vs 關掉 moves a whole sentence.
3. **Sentence length.** Short fragments read spoken; long subordinate chains read written.
4. **English mixing.** Normal in HK speech at every level except L1. Purging it sounds stilted;
   overloading it sounds like a consultant deck.
5. **The 「最X係」 filler**, as above.

## A note for people adapting this

The level boundaries here are one defensible cut, not a standard. If your voice puts 黐線 at L2,
change the table — copy it into your own profile rather than arguing with the skill. The two
tests are the part worth keeping; the word lists are illustration.
